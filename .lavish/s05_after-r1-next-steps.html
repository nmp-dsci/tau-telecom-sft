01 · your two questions

Short answers first

Is fine-tuning the 4B a dead end, so we need a better model?

No. But more of the same data is.

  • Fine-tuning worked: 6 → 17 on test, with 13 tasks fixed and 2 broken. On mobile data, R1's 11/14 beats Claude v2's 9/14.
  • The same recipe has stalled on MMS: on its own training tasks R1 passes MMS 6 of 32 times, with about 6.6 teacher conversations per task. More clean demonstrations of the same tasks won't fix that.
  • What's missing is behaviour: it needs data from the states it actually gets into (a customer refusing, a tool error, a fix that didn't work), or a training signal that punishes repetition.
  • Size is still an open question: 4B may be too small for 30-turn troubleshooting. M6, the 30B, is the test of that, and it's already planned.
Is the agent a ReAct loop? Which framework?

An act–observe loop with native function calling, but no written "Thought" step.

  • Harness: tau2-bench's own orchestrator (half duplex), the same one the leaderboard runs, drives tau2-loop's LoopAgent. There's no LangChain or similar framework.
  • Who wrote it: LoopAgent was written by Claude Code in this portfolio: all 9 commits to factory.py are co-authored by Claude. For a student it does what tau2's stock llm_agent does: the same prompt bytes, the same generate() call with the tools, and the same limits of 200 messages and 10 errors. That stock agent is the one the board's GLM-5 and Qwen3.5 runs, our teachers, record. The part that differs from the board is the customer (§02's table).
  • Each step is one call: tau2's generate() calls litellm.completion(messages, tools=13, tool_choice="auto"). That goes to the recording proxy, then to vLLM on Modal, which parses <tool_call> into structured tool_calls with its hermes parser.
  • Routing: a reply with tool calls goes to the environment and the results come back. A reply with only text goes to the customer. Nothing parses a written Thought/Action/Observation.
  • Claude v2 uses the same harness by a different route: the Claude Agent SDK, with the tools described in the prompt as a JSON reply format, and the 37-line checklist that s4 doesn't have (§05).
02 · how the agent runs

One conversation: the orchestrator routes each message by its shape

Every step sends the whole history with the 13 tool schemas and gets back one message. That's the ReAct pattern of act, observe, act, without a written reasoning step and without any guard against repeating.

  1. No Thought step. The served model replies with tool calls, text, or both. Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 is the non-thinking variant, and the teacher conversations had their reasoning stripped in M1.
  2. No loop guard and no trimming. state.system_messages + state.messages is resent in full every step (factory.py:281). Only three things end a call: the customer stopping, 200 messages (max_steps, which counts every message, not agent turns), or 10 tool errors across the conversation (max_errors, tau2's default).
  3. Routing. is_tool_call means tool_calls is not None, so a message with text and calls goes to the environment (orchestrator.py:869-872). s4 samples at temperature 0.7, top_p 0.8, top_k 20 (agents/telecom/s4/agent.yaml:9-11).

96% of test tool calls name the agent's own 13 tools (R0: 59%). 3,286 replies carried tool_calls and only 29 wrote a call as text. The prompt bytes matched on all 6,113 requests.

The first fixes land

Even in conversations it failed, R1 enabled roaming 28 of 28 times, had airplane mode toggled 26/32 and network mode set 23/34. It starts the troubleshooting tree correctly.

Efficient when it passes

Its passing conversations take a median 18 agent turns on test (train 24). The teachers took a median 14. Claude v2's median is 23.5 over all of its conversations.

When the loops start

A loop starts at the first agent action after which the next 6 actions all repeat earlier ones. It matched 12 of the 13 test max_steps runs and 27 of the 29 train ones. Each dot is one conversation, and the red bar is the median.

testtrainmedian: turn 38 test, 29 train

The loops start where the teacher data runs out. Only 3 of the 518 teacher conversations reach 29 agent turns (0.6%), and none go past 32. Past that point R1 has never seen a demonstration.

Context length is not the cause. Most loops start at 12–14K tokens, inside the lengths M4 trained on (8.2K–16.9K). 29% of all served requests run past 16.9K, but by then the loop has usually started.

05 · why

Four causes, ranked by how much evidence each has

1 · It never saw a conversation go wrong and recover

strong evidence

v1.1 keeps only clean passes. M1's filters dropped every conversation with an agent tool error (F5), every unusually long one (F6) and every one that called a customer's tool (F4). The teachers faced a cooperative customer and finished in a median 14 turns. So R1 has never seen a refusal, an error, or a fix that didn't work, followed by what to do next. Its loops start in exactly those states, past turn 29, where only 0.6% of the demonstrations reach. This is behaviour cloning's known weakness: small mistakes compound into states the demonstrations never visited.

2 · The customer changed between training and scoring

medium

The training conversations used gpt-5.2 at low effort under tau2's own rules (the card's source table). Scoring uses Haiku 5.5 at high effort under tau2_loop/2, a customer that refuses to rerun checks, asks "what next?" and disputes charges, as the quotes in §04 show. Claude v2 takes a median 23.5 turns against this customer; the teachers took 14 against theirs.

3 · Claude v2 has a checklist that s4 doesn't

medium

v2's prompt carries a 37-line <technical_support_checklist>. It covers both roaming switches, get_data_usage then refuel, granting both app permissions, and no transfer before every check. Those are nearly the exact steps R1 misses. s4 runs tau2's stock prompt, like its teachers. So "R1 vs Claude v2" is partly stock prompt against tuned prompt. The checklist was written by tau2-loop's Sonnet optimiser, so it's Claude's text.

4 · 4B may be too small for 30-turn troubleshooting

unknown

We can't separate this from causes 1–3 yet. The talk's Qwen3 30B reached 75% after SFT, on a different 40 tasks. M6 (Qwen3-30B-A3B, R2 untrained and R3 tuned) is the direct test. R1's in-sample MMS score of 6/32 is consistent both with too little capacity and with cause 1.

06 · options

What each option tests, and what it costs

Costs are Modal's L4 list price times the wall time measured in M4 and M5, unless marked. The Haiku customer runs on the Claude subscription. Any paid run needs one measured conversation or step first.

A · Diagnose first

recommend
  • A1, the checklist at inference only. s5 = s4 plus v2's checklist, with no retraining, on the 40 test tasks. If MMS jumps, the gap is the policy knowledge, and the data should carry it.
  • A2, three more R1 test trials. That gives pass^3 for the success criterion, and the noise around 17/40.
testscauses 3 and 4, and the noisecost≈ $0.85 + ≈ $2.40 · about 4 h of runs · no training

B · Data v2: recovery from R1's own states

the main fix
  • Play R1 on the 74 training tasks, 4 trials each. Find where it loops or hits an error, and let an open teacher (GLM-5 or Qwen3.5-397B) carry on from that exact state. This is DAgger-style correction.
  • Keep only the continuations that pass. Put loss on the teacher's turns only; R1's turns are context with no loss.
  • Also keep teacher conversations that recover from a tool error, which v1 dropped. Then continue training M4's adapter on them (G).
testscauses 1 and 2costR1 rollouts ≈ $5.20 · teacher and customer API: measured first · training ≈ 2–4 h, ≈ $2.50–4.50 (G)

C · A preference step after B

add-on

DPO on the states where R1 loops. Rejected is the repeated call; chosen is the teacher's next step from B. It directly penalises repeating, which SFT on clean demonstrations can't do.

testscause 1, targetedcostreuses B's states · about 1–2 h on the L4

G · Continue training the M4 adapter

yes: how B and C would train

Yes, the adapter can be trained further. Load M4's LoRA weights (sha256 8956c198…) as trainable and carry on with new rows, rather than starting a fresh LoRA. That's cheaper and faster to iterate, and C's DPO step starts from it anyway.

  • Mix in v1.1. Training on the new recovery rows alone would wear away what already works on mobile data and service.
  • Use a lower learning rate (about 5e-5) for about 1 epoch. Optimizer state isn't carried over, because the data is different.
  • Trainer change: an init_adapter field in the config. Its sha256 is checked against M4's run.json and recorded in the new run, so the R row names both its parent and its data.
costabout 1 epoch over v1.1 plus the new rows: ≈ 2–4 h, ≈ $2.50–4.50 on the L4, against ≈ 7–11 h to retrain from scratchtrade-offretrain from scratch only if the results table should show one clean recipe

E · RL on tau2's reward

later

GRPO-style training on whole conversations is the strongest fix for long horizons. It's also the most engineering: a reward server, rollouts with a customer, and many GPU hours. It's worth it only after B or D says the 4B, or the 30B, is close.

F · A loop guard in the harness

not recommended

Detect a repeated call and tell the model. It's cheap and would likely win a few tasks. But it changes the agent, not the model, so the R rows would stop being comparable with Claude v2's harness. It's only useful as a diagnostic.

07 · the plan

Diagnose cheaply, then fix the data, with the 30B running beside

Phase 1 · diagnose (A1, A2, and R2 from D). Make s5 in the tau2-loop worktree and play it once on test. Play R1 three more times on test. Serve the untrained 30B and play it on test once (R2). ≈ $6 · no training · the gate: does the checklist lift MMS by 4 or more? Is R2 already near R1?
Phase 2 · data v2 (B, then C). R1 rollouts on the 74 training tasks, teacher continuations from loop and error states, and the leak check. Build v2's card and sign it off. Continue training M4's adapter on v1.1 plus the new rows (G), which gives R4 in the results table. Then optionally the DPO step from that adapter. the customer and the checklist are decisions 2 and 3 below · paid parts measured first
Phase 3 · M6 with the better data. Train the 30B on whichever dataset wins (R3), and score it like R1, with the sign test against R2. A100 80 GB · measured step first
What stays fixed: no test task or twin in anything the trainer reads (the leak check runs on v2 too). Agent turns come only from open-weights models. tau2-loop changes go in the worktree only. Every paid run follows a measured step. 1 trial per task unless you choose decision 1's A2.
